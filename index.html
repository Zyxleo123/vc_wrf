Weak-responder fraction

Every plot from the chemCPA and CellFlow comparison, with the axes and the marks written out. Test numbers below are means over the 17 held-out doses. Model numbers are then means over three seeds.

At the primary threshold, chemCPA’s absolute error is 7.1 percentage points with no weak-responder penalty, 5.3 with three thresholds, and 6.6 with thirteen. CellFlow is 5.3, 4.9, and 4.9 on the same three settings. Log-dose interpolation, which predicts only the fraction, is lower than all of them. The plots below are what those averages leave out: which dose moved, whether the body of the score moved, and whether expression outside the proliferation genes paid for the fraction.

How to read a score

Response score, r or t
How far a cell’s proliferation-program activity sits below the vehicle mean, in vehicle standard deviations. The program is the Tirosh 2016 S and G2/M gene set, 97 genes. Activity is the log of one plus the sum of the unlogged program counts. Larger score means stronger suppression of that program. The vehicle mean and scale are fixed from control cells, so every condition uses the same ruler.
Threshold τ
A vertical cut on that ruler. A weak responder is a cell to the left of the cut. The primary cut is the 0.95 quantile of vehicle scores, τ ≈ 1.74. Two other cuts used in training sit at the 0.90 and 0.975 vehicle quantiles. The thirteen-threshold runs use a wider grid of vehicle quantiles.
The spike near 3.52
Cells with zero counts on every program gene. That is the strongest suppression the score can record, and it sits to the right of every training threshold. A model can change the fraction below τ by creating this spike while leaving the rest of the cells where they were.
Weak-responder fraction
The share of cells with score below τ. Absolute error is the absolute gap between the predicted share and the measured share, in percentage points. Signed error is predicted share minus measured share, also in percentage points. Positive signed error means too many cells were placed below the cut.
Held-out dose
The test split is 17 drug–dose pairs, one dose per drug, either 100 nM or 1000 nM. Validation is a separate 9 doses and is used only for the penalty-weight plots and the matched three-versus-thirteen comparison.
  • No weak-responder penalty
  • Penalty at 3 thresholds
  • Penalty at 13 thresholds
  • MMD penalty (CellFlow only)
  • A reference that is not a trained model

chemCPA checkpoints are the epoch with the lowest validation MMD². CellFlow’s baseline and MMD runs were chosen from checkpoints between step 500 and 30,000. The CellFlow weak-responder runs were chosen only from steps 5,000 to 30,000. That difference in the candidate range sits inside every CellFlow comparison.

Where the average sits

Each row is one method. The rows are sorted so the smallest value is at the top. Lower is better on every plot in this section. A small dot is one seed: that seed’s average over the 17 test doses. The large dot is the mean of the seeds. Grey rows are references and have a single dot, because they are not retrained across seeds.

Log-dose interpolation, the adjacent-dose mixture, the nearest training dose, vehicle cells, and replicate wells appear when the metric is defined for them. Interpolation predicts a fraction from neighboring training doses of the same drug, so it is absent from Wasserstein, MMD, and gene-mean plots. The Gaussian chemCPA decoder is not on these strips. It predicts a weak-responder fraction of 100% on every test dose.

Signed error

Signed error is predicted weak-responder fraction minus measured fraction, in percentage points, at τ₀.₉₅. Positive means the model called too many cells weak responders. Absolute error hides this direction: a dose that is 10 points high and a dose that is 10 points low both contribute 10 to the average absolute error and cancel in the signed average.

The penalty weight, on validation

These plots are chemCPA on the 9 validation doses. The horizontal axis is λ, the weight on the weak-responder penalty, on a logarithmic scale. The penalty is averaged over the thresholds in the run, so adding thresholds does not by itself multiply λ. h is the bandwidth of that penalty. The vertical bars are the standard deviation across three seeds. A solid line is bandwidth 0.5. A dashed line is another bandwidth. Orange is three thresholds. Green is thirteen. The blue horizontal line is chemCPA with no penalty (λ = 0). The Gaussian decoder is omitted. The test runs reported everywhere else use λ = 10.

Interpolation and the noise under the metric

Beating the neural baseline is a different claim from beating a fraction copied from the neighboring training doses. The noise plot says how large a gain has to be before it clears experimental disagreement.

Log-dose interpolation against the three-threshold models

Axes

Horizontal
Absolute error of log-dose interpolation at τ₀.₉₅, percentage points. Interpolation is a weighted average of the measured fractions at the neighboring training doses of the same drug, with the weight linear in log dose. It predicts the fraction and no expression.
Vertical
Absolute error of the model at the same threshold and the same dose.

What the marks mean

Orange circles
chemCPA with the three-threshold penalty. The figure legend says chemCPA. The points are that run, not the baseline and not the thirteen-threshold run.
Blue squares
CellFlow with the three-threshold penalty. The figure legend says CellFlow. Same reading: this series is the three-threshold run.
Grey line
Equal error. A point above the line is a dose where interpolation is closer than the model. A point below the line is a dose the model wins.
One point
One held-out dose.

What this plot is for

The cheap fraction estimate, dose by dose. chemCPA’s three-threshold run is about 1.7 points worse than interpolation on average, and the dose interval on that gap excludes zero. It wins 3 of 17 doses.

Measurement noise and model error

Axes

Horizontal
Threshold quantile.
Vertical
Mean absolute difference in the weak-responder fraction, percentage points.

What the marks mean

Grey line with crosses
Replicate wells: two measurements of the same condition. This is experimental disagreement, about 3.8 points at quantile 0.95 and about 10 points in the middle of the curve.
Black line
A random split of the same cells. This misses plate and biology noise, so it sits below the replicate line.
Grey dotted line
Binomial fluctuation of a perfect generator that draws 1,024 cells and has the true fraction. A lower bound from counting noise.
Orange solid line
chemCPA, three-threshold penalty, mean absolute error on the test doses.
Blue dashed line
CellFlow, three-threshold penalty, on the thirteen-quantile grid.

What this plot is for

The scale for every gain in this report. Model error above the replicate curve is misspecification. A CellFlow gain of about 0.4 points is about a tenth of the replicate gap at the primary threshold. Redrawing chemCPA’s cells moves a dose’s fraction by under a point at that threshold, which is small next to the replicate gap and next to the spread across doses.

Test split unless a figure title or a row label says validation. chemCPA fractions and expression come from local generated cells. CellFlow fractions, Wasserstein distances, and gene-mean errors come from the published evaluation tables. Adjacent-dose mixtures use real cells from a neighboring dose and can be scored on expression. Interpolation cannot.
